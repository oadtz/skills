# Generation and evidence

## Framing and lanes
Use several genuinely different starting points: unmet desire, costly workaround, strange behavior, changed capability, revived failed concept, cross-industry mechanism, identity/emotion or a physical resource mismatch. Do not force every frame into autonomy. Parallel agents are optional only when authorized; otherwise use separate passes and disclose their shared context. Neither provider variety nor role-play guarantees independent thinking.

Default six slots: two wild Explore, two mechanism Exploit and two Mutation/crossbreed. Evolution is the enclosing selection-and-feedback loop, not an additional slot allocation. Lane counts may change within the taste-informed portion. Preserve two wild candidates through the final shortlist; do not reject them merely because predicted taste is low. For N candidates choose integer W nearest 0.35N within 0.30N–0.40N if possible; otherwise nearest integer and report the achieved percentage. A direct user override changes the allocation and is logged explicitly.

Wild seeds use brief and constraints without favorite names, score-derived mechanisms or taste-based ranking. They may coincidentally overlap taste; document how they arose. In a context already exposed to taste, call the lane taste-unfiltered rather than blind. For stronger independence use an authorized fresh context containing only the brief.

## Mechanism construction and transformation
Candidate card: `ID | lane | usage scene | actor/action/outcome | changed mechanism | beneficiary and payer hypothesis | parent IDs/operator/delta (or independent seed) | facts/inferences/speculation | biggest uncertainty | falsification test | user score (null until supplied)`.

Exploit examines why an outcome matters, its wedge and missing enabling conditions. Mutation changes one or more causal elements: remove a weakness, change resource ownership, reverse buyer/seller, remove the interface, change who bears risk, move to physical delivery or change payment to realized outcomes. Crossbreeding combines compatible mechanisms, with an explicit account of why their interaction creates value. Names, character skins and industry substitutions alone are not evolution.

Illustrative unvalidated example: combine an independent seed about routing unused resources with another seed about temporary coordination. A venue with idle weekday space offers a bounded package assembled from existing local instructors and equipment; the service dissolves after delivery. Parent IDs: the two saved seed IDs; operator: combine resource routing with temporary coordination. Delta: the customer buys a completed event, rather than a listing or a project plan. Demand, permissions and delivery margin remain unverified. This is an explanation of a transformation, not a scored historical idea or a recommended startup.

## Anti-AI-slop review
Before retaining a candidate, inspect:
- Can a person explain what the customer does and receives without “AI-powered”, “platform”, “ecosystem” or invented jargon?
- What becomes possible, or materially more useful, because a constraint changed? If AI is removable without affecting value, describe the actual business honestly.
- Does the proposed autonomy yield a valued outcome, or just activity and anthropomorphism?
- Is the claimed novelty a new causal mechanism, a meaningful combination, or only a renamed incumbent?
- What existing substitute already solves enough of the problem? What would cause rejection?

Do not impose a permanent ban on SaaS, B2B, games, infrastructure or non-AI businesses. The historical one-run PET/game ban was an exploration experiment, not a lifelong preference.

## Web research
Initial divergence precedes competitor-led filtering. Lightweight capability/behavior research can seed exploration; substantial market attack follows a shortlist. Verify unstable facts before presenting them as current even during early framing.

For each promising candidate investigate direct competitors, substitutes/manual workarounds, who currently pays whom, evidence of repeated demand, enabling technology limits, access/cost constraints, timing, and incumbent incentives. Prefer official product documentation, original research and direct customer evidence. Provider marketing establishes what is advertised, not independent efficacy or demand. Compare actual functionality, customer, geography and business model; “not found” never means “does not exist.”

Evidence entries include claim, direct URL, source title, publication date when available, access date, supporting observation, limits and status: `verified fact`, `inference`, `speculation`, or `unverified`. Label the reasoning bridge from a capability to a proposed business. Research can demote, reshape or reject an idea; preserve that outcome in the run. Historical links must be reopened before reuse as current evidence. Do not copy unresolved citation tokens as citations.

When browsing is unavailable or the user excludes it, continue with clearly unverified concepts and specify what needs checking. Do not imply a completed reality attack. Never invent market size, competitor absence, technical readiness, prices, citations or user scores.

## Rationalizations and red flags
| Temptation | Correction |
|---|---|
| “Three favorites scored 9; replay them to maximize fit.” | Extract mechanisms; record a material behavioral delta and keep the wild allocation. |
| “We already invested hours in these hybrids.” | Sunk effort does not turn combined names into new mechanisms. |
| “Pitch deadline means claims must sound certain.” | Present proposed differentiation and explicit unknowns; evidence status does not change with urgency. |
| “Memory is in the chat, so the next session will know.” | Write a run record and resume pointer; disclose write failures. |

Red flags: every idea resembles the same parent; wild ideas removed for low taste fit; assistant scores treated as user feedback; harms generalized from one label; research that only confirms; new capability presented as proven demand. Repair the specific failure before finalizing the run.
